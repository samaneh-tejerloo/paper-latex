\begin{abstract}
	Identifying functional modules in protein-protein interaction (PPI) networks is essential for understanding cellular organization and biological processes. However, the sparse and heterogeneous topology of PPI networks, together with the overlapping and nested organization of protein complexes, poses significant challenges to accurate module identification. In this study, we propose XXXX, an end-to-end learning framework based on graph neural networks that combines PPI network topology with complementary protein functional information derived from Gene Ontology (GO). The framework incorporates extensions of the NOCD loss function and exploits binary GO features and GO2Vec embeddings. Furthermore, an aggregated thresholding strategy is introduced to improve the recovery of overlapping and nested protein complexes. Comprehensive experiments on five yeast PPI datasets demonstrate that the proposed method consistently outperforms state-of-the-art approaches, highlighting the effectiveness of the proposed method.
\end{abstract}

\textbf{keywords:} Protein--protein interaction networks, Functional module identification, Graph neural networks, Gene Ontology, Protein complex detection, Overlapping communities
